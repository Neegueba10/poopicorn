Model

—

View

Drag the model to move the selected part, in the plane facing you. Drag the background — or shift-drag — to orbit. The camera is orthographic, so a drag is exact.
Wheel zooms the picture, middle-drag or space-drag pans it, f fits it to the pane. That is the viewport, not the camera: it changes nothing in the file, and it only ever scales by whole numbers.

Materials

Click a swatch to paint the selected part with it. Double-click to change what that colour is — picked against the real palette, because a colour the palette cannot represent is a colour the render will not produce. Shift-click deletes it.

Packing

Wheel zooms, middle-drag or space-drag pans, f fits. Whole-number scales only, so a sprite's pixels stay square.
The layout is not saved: both bakers shelf-pack from the sizes at boot, so a sheet file has no x/y in it. Width is what the game passes to sheetBaker; ids and sizes are what the file fixes.

Timing

A clip is one model and a dense table of numbers: one value per animated field per frame. Nothing interpolates at run time, so easing and holds cost the game zero bytes — the tool writes the samples.
fps and mode are for the game to read; neither changes what is baked. March cost is per part per pixel and multiplies by the frame count, so watch the estimate above.

Frame

Move a part with the gizmo and it is keyed at the playhead — every number that moves becomes a channel. There is no record button, because a record button is a mode you forget you are not in.
A number here is that field's value at this frame. Scrub it and only this frame bakes again. × stops animating the field and hands it back to the model.
kind cannot be animated, and neither can a scatter header: its seed and spreads decide the arrangement, and a mass that reshuffles per frame is confetti. Animate the template under it and the whole mass moves together.

Rig

A part rotates about its own centre, so an arm of three parts cannot swing from a shoulder by rotating each of them. A group is a pivot and a list of parts: turn it and the tool writes each part's new centre and rotation into channels.
The group is the editor's. It resolves into plain numbers the moment you turn it, so the format gains no hierarchy and the game pays nothing — the pivot is kept only so you can grab it again.
The orange square on the picture is the pivot. place gives it arms to move it — moving a pivot moves no part — and turn gives it rings, in world axes. It cannot bend or squash a part; a group moves rigid pieces.

Sprites

The index is the sprite id and it is permanent: deleting leaves a tombstone (0 in the file) that renders nothing, so a game referencing sprite 17 keeps meaning it.

Sprite

A dial shown dim is inherited from the model's own view; edit it to override, ↺ to inherit again. Eight facings are eight slots holding one number each.
Drag a sprite to turn it, the cross at the bottom left to turn or pan it, and — with lights on — either sun to aim it. The same gestures as the model tab, writing to this slot instead of to the model.
A light aimed here lights one sprite. That is what you want for a prop and wrong for a facing set, where the key would jump as the character turns — aim it once, then apply to set.

Widget

Drag an arm to act on that axis, or the white dot to move in the plane facing you — which is how a paint-only part is moved, since it has no surface to click. Arms are in the part's OWN frame, and shape edits the dimension the primitive reads rather than any transform. Shift-drag for a slow one. The cross at the bottom left turns the camera; its middle pans.
shape also draws the part's cage — the box its shape actually fills, measured with the same support function that crops sprites in the sheet tab. It is the only picture of a paint-only part, which has no surface, and of a subtract part, where you otherwise see the hole and never the cutter. A cut's cage is warm; everything else is blue. A paint part's cage includes its reach, since that is how far its colour goes.
On the cage: a diamond dilates — it writes the primitive's round, the corner or radius no arm reaches. A square moves one face, and the opposite face stays put: the drag is split between the dimension and the part's position, which is what makes the box adjustable rather than merely scalable. Alt-drag resizes about the centre instead. Faces are only offered where a shape really has two of them — a box and an ellipsoid get all six, a cylinder, cone or prism gets its two end caps, a pyramid gets its base, and a sphere or torus gets none, because there the faces would be one number pretending to be several. A capsule gets two rings on its joints instead: drag one to aim the limb and the other stays put. Its a b c is an axis, not a box, so it has ends rather than faces — and dragging one writes no rotation.
lights puts a sun on each light, and a sun stays under the pointer as you drag it. Past the silhouette it rides the rim. A drag keeps the side the light is on — flip front / back in the View panel sends it through to the other side without moving it on screen, which is how a rim light is made. A sun goes dim when it is back there. They are hidden by default, so they never sit over a part you meant to grab.

Parts

Order decides the shape: a part blends with everything before it, and a subtract only removes what is already there. Delete removes the selected part.
Shift-click a row to select several parts — Ctrl-click a part in the picture does the same, since shift there already orbits. The widget then moves, turns and scales them together, about their own centre and about the WORLD's axes. Scale is uniform: a per-axis one would push a world axis into numbers that live in each part's own frame. Escape goes back to one part.
mirror x ⇔ folds the sample across x = 0, so one row is a symmetric pair and costs no extra numbers. A part crossing the plane folds into a chevron — a pair of horns from one row. x is held at 0 or above: on the far side there is no half left to mirror.
bake mirror spends a row to turn the fold into a real second part, placed and rotated to match. Do it when the two sides have to move apart: a clip channel writes one row, so a mirrored pair can only ever be animated in step.
scatter ⁘ is two rows — a header, and the template under it that the header copies. The copies are made at render time, so forty pebbles are 26 numbers. The template's own flags reach every copy: subtract for a field of pits, paint for stains, mirror for a symmetric mass. 🎲 rerolls the arrangement. The pair moves, duplicates and deletes as one. Watch the second number in the status line — march time is linear in it.

Part

Drag any number sideways to scrub it — Shift ×10, Alt ÷10 — or click it to type: Enter commits, Escape puts the old number back. blend is how softly this part melts into the ones before it; 0 is a hard join.
